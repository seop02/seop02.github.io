About

I am an AI/quant researcher at Qraft Technologies, currently serving in the South Korean alternative military service program. My current work focuses on high-frequency ETF arbitrage, at the intersection of statistical modelling, machine learning, and low-latency engineering, mostly in C++, Rust, and Python. Before this, I developed reinforcement-learning-based order execution algorithms for derivatives markets.

I did my master's (MMathPhys) at the University of Oxford, where I worked on quantum algorithms; my thesis work with Bálint Koczor on preparing ground and excited states via adiabatic CoVaR was published in New Journal of Physics.

Research Interests

I am drawn to a single broad problem: finding and exploiting structure in complex systems — identifying what genuinely carries signal, modelling the dynamics that generate it, and acting on that understanding under uncertainty. The recurring lesson, in both my research and my trading work, is that representation decides tractability: the right basis, ansatz, or invariant can render an otherwise hopeless problem solvable, whether that means isolating signal from microstructure noise in markets or preparing eigenstates on near-term quantum hardware.

Markets and quantum systems are, for me, two instances of this same question — noisy, high-dimensional systems where the structure you can extract determines what you can compute. My current work develops the statistical and engineering side of that toolkit; my ongoing interest in quantum algorithms pursues its sharpest form: what structure a quantum device can actually exploit, and at what cost in circuit depth and samples.
